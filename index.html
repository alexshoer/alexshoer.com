Who I am

I left banking on a hunch, moved to Shanghai, and never looked back.

In 2011 I walked away from a career in finance to work on clean energy. China had the most ambitious environmental goals in the world, and coal was choking its cities, so I moved to Shanghai to be where the problem was biggest. I stayed nine years, learned Mandarin, and built a company that helped put solar on factory rooftops across the country.

Today I’m back in the Bay Area, living in Sausalito and building GridVest, which helps developers and EPCs get battery storage projects built. The through-line hasn’t changed: more clean energy, deployed faster, at a cost that makes sense.

Closer to home, I serve on the City of Sausalito’s Sustainability Commission and as Vice President of the Floating Home Association. Living on the water has shown me what a close community can be, and I’m increasingly interested in how more of us could live that way: sharing more, relying on each other more, and building places where people genuinely show up for one another.

What it taught me about the US and China

Stop refighting a battle we’ve already lost. Win the next one.

China has won the battery cell. Trying to beat it at that game is one of the biggest drags on US deployment today. The real prize is the integrated system, the next technology, and the clean power buildout that decides whether America can power AI and secure its own energy future.

01Treat the cell as a commodityUse the world’s best cells to deploy faster. The value and the IP sit in integration: the battery management, inverters, controls, and software around the cell.
02Partner where we can’t winBring manufacturing onshore through licensing and joint ventures, like Ford and CATL in Michigan: US jobs, a US tax base, and know-how we can’t build overnight.
03Leapfrog where we canPut capital behind what comes next, like solid-state and long-duration storage, where America’s research and risk-taking still give it a real head start.

Energy independence is no longer just a climate goal. It’s the foundation for powering AI, for feeding ourselves as food production electrifies, and for a foreign policy that’s no longer bent around securing oil. Hear me make the case on Cleaning Up →
